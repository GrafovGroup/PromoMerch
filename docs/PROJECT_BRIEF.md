## 3. Offer and customer experience

Focus the launch on three understandable offers:

- **Team apparel:** shirts, polos, hoodies, outerwear, caps, and toques.
- **Event merchandise:** drinkware, bags, lanyards, giveaways, and staff clothing.
- **Employee kits and gifts:** onboarding, recognition, corporate gifting, and coordinated packs.

Support browsing by product and purpose, a multi-product quote shortlist, a “help me choose” enquiry, and repeat-order requests. Use **REQUEST A QUOTE** as the principal action. Do not imply live checkout, automatic pricing, stock availability, or guaranteed fulfilment until those functions and facts are supported.

The quote journey should collect contact details, product/brief, quantities, deadline or flexible timing, destination postal code, optional budget, and optional artwork. Make total-project versus per-item budgets explicit. Kits need recipient quantities and consolidated versus multiple-destination delivery; reorders need a previous order or quote reference. Preserve the chosen language and shortlist across the journey.

Explain the workflow: brief, product selection and quote, artwork approval, production, delivery. Present company stores as an enquiry-led capability only if supportable; do not advertise a functioning portal that has not been built.

## 4. Data, content, and claims

Use maintainable bilingual product records with stable IDs, descriptions, specifications, images, colours/sizes, and verified decoration options. Track evidence and verification dates privately. Do not invent supplier relationships, SKUs, prices, minimum quantities, lead times, inventory, certifications, sustainability claims, customer logos, testimonials, or office locations.

Partner delivery is compatible with direct-provider positioning. Do not claim owned factories, warehouses, decorators, or a national branch network without evidence. Build useful category, service, industry, and guidance pages before expanding geographic coverage. Publish location pages only when they add substantive local information; avoid mass city-name substitutions.

Apply universal standards for bilingual navigation, French in the top bar, uppercase menus/buttons, responsive wide layouts, relevant imagery, accessible forms, performance, favicon coverage, canonical URLs, hreflang, and factual structured data. No public implementation notes, AI credits, fabricated reviews, or unfinished placeholders.

## 5. Launch checks and acceptance

The later build may proceed with documented configuration placeholders, but public launch requires:

- Confirmed domain and hosting control, functioning new-brand phone/email, and the actual legal seller/contact details.
- A real form endpoint with bilingual validation, spam protection, secure optional uploads, honest success/failure states, and verified test delivery.
- Verified product claims and imagery rights, complete French content, and mobile/accessibility checks.
- An approved migration map and email/DNS continuity plan before redirect activation; see `MERCHMATTERS_MIGRATION.md`.

The legacy homepage currently states that Merch Matters is a brand of Facility Unlimited Corp. Preserve that legal entity unless the user explicitly approves a change. Its listed Toronto address and legacy contacts are historical public statements, not proof of PromoMerch premises or operational new-brand channels.
