## 3. Business proposition and commercial launch model

Position PromoMerch as the customer's direct branded merchandise provider: helping the buyer select suitable products, arrange branding and packaging, and coordinate delivery. Partners may perform sourcing, decoration, assembly or shipping where those arrangements are real. Do not describe the business as a directory, referral broker or marketplace.

Primary proposition:

> Branded merchandise for Canadian businesses, teams and events.

The practical value is product selection around the audience, quantity, budget and delivery requirement, followed by a clear quote and artwork approval process. Demonstrate that value in the experience instead of claiming “Canada's leading,” guaranteed savings or unlimited capacity.

Prioritize three buying needs:

1. **Team apparel:** T-shirts, polos, hoodies, outerwear, caps and toques.
2. **Event merchandise:** drinkware, bags, lanyards, giveaways and event staff clothing.
3. **Employee kits and gifts:** onboarding packs, recognition gifts, corporate gifting and coordinated merchandise kits.

Other useful product families can include office items, stationery and technology accessories when supported by real sourcing. The earlier planning target of **30–50 curated products** is a directional merchandising goal, not an instruction to fabricate catalogue records. A useful verified smaller catalogue or honest product-family catalogue is preferable to invented stock.

Launch with a **quote-led catalogue**:

- Primary action: **REQUEST A QUOTE**.
- Secondary actions: **EXPLORE PRODUCTS**, **ADD TO QUOTE**, **PLAN A MERCH PROJECT**, and contextual phone contact.
- Provide a multi-product quote shortlist, project-brief enquiry and reorder path.
- Explain quote factors such as quantities, decoration, setup, packaging, kitting, freight and applicable taxes without inventing fixed rates or public policies.
- Do not build checkout, payment processing, live inventory, an account system or automatic order pricing without approved real data and a reason to add them.
- Company stores and ongoing merchandise programmes may be an enquiry-led service if actually supportable. Do not show a functioning store-management portal or imply one exists before it does.

Prioritize corporate teams, commercial/industrial employers, event organizers, schools/institutions, associations, nonprofits and clubs based on actual capability. Do not make unverified public-sector registration, Indigenous procurement, safety-product, certification or brand-authorization claims.

## 9. Catalogue data, product integrity and quote shortlist

The catalogue is a customer decision aid. It must not imply purchasable stock or exact specifications when only a product family has been established.

Distinguish two record types:

- **Verified product:** identifiable, sourced product with permissioned imagery and evidence for published attributes.
- **Product family/example:** general merchandise type or illustrative collection, with honest descriptive content and a request-for-options action. No invented supplier SKU, precise stock or fake technical specifications.

Recommended maintainable record fields:

```text
id
recordType: verified_product | product_family
status: draft | published
indexable: boolean
categoryIds
useCaseIds
locales.en-CA: slug, title, summary, description, metadata, FAQs
locales.fr-CA: equivalent localized content
images: asset path, alt by locale, role, illustration/product-photo classification
availableOptions: supported colours, sizes, materials and decoration options
specifications: sourced facts only
minimumQuantity: verified number or null
leadTime: verified wording/context or null
price: verified public price with currency/basis or null
quoteOnly: true by default
relatedProductIds
publicDownloads: authorized useful documents
lastContentReview
```

Keep supplier account details, costs, private price lists, confidential terms, verification notes and contact records outside public client bundles and public Git history. A private source/claims record can reference internal evidence without copying confidential source material into this public repository.

Do not fabricate stock, exact lead times, minimum quantities, certifications, recycled percentages, “eco-friendly” claims, brand authorizations, printing limitations, colours or sizes. Unknown fields are null or omitted, not zero, “unlimited” or silently guessed. Do not repeat supplier copy at scale without adding useful selection/context and respecting usage rights.

Public verified product details can include materials, measurements, sizes/colours, decoration choices, imprint areas, artwork requirements, packaging and care guidance where sourced. For family pages, explain choices without presenting illustrative images as guaranteed SKU variants.

### Shortlist behaviour

- ADD TO QUOTE adds a stable product/family ID and quantity intent, with useful visual confirmation.
- Let customers add several items, edit quantities, remove lines and continue browsing.
- Support an empty state and a “help me choose” path.
- Carry selected IDs, quantities and context into the real quote request and notification.
- Preserve a shortlist across locale switching using stable IDs. Store only the minimum non-sensitive shortlist state; no artwork or personal enquiry text in public URLs.
- Do not calculate order totals, shipping, tax, inventory or guaranteed delivery without a verified supported data source.
- Make all controls keyboard accessible and expose count/status changes appropriately.
- Validate submitted IDs and quantity values server-side; do not trust arbitrary hidden inputs.
- Handle stale/removed records gracefully rather than submitting broken names or discarding the whole enquiry.

A simple searchable/filterable catalogue can be useful if the actual catalogue warrants it. Filters must work, show clear empty results and avoid generating indexable faceted URL combinations. Do not build an elaborate marketplace interface for a small curated selection.

Build three connected flows using one maintainable backend/schema where possible:

1. **Product shortlist:** selected items and approximate quantities.
2. **Project brief:** customer describes the audience, purpose and timing and asks for selection help.
3. **Reorder:** prior order/quote reference and requested changes; no forced customer account.

Keep the first step short. Collect only what helps the team respond; show additional fields progressively.

| Field | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Name | Required |
| Email or another supported reliable reply method | Require a usable response route |
| Company/organization | Useful; do not exclude teams or smaller buyers unnecessarily |
| Phone | Optional unless selected as the necessary reply method |
| Product shortlist or project description | At least one meaningful scope input |
| Quantity | Approximate quantity or “help me estimate”; per line where relevant |
| Delivery postal code | Useful for an actual quote; handle international requests honestly if not supported |

| Artwork | Optional supported secure upload or a real alternative |

| Language | Set from active locale, allow preferred reply language |
| Reorder reference | Conditional; do not expose other customers' orders |
| Kits | Conditional recipient count, kit brief and single/multiple destination indicator |

### Functional requirements

- Accessible labels, autocomplete, visible required/optional states, client and server validation, field-specific errors and a form summary when helpful.
- Correct French validation, confirmations, notifications and privacy text.
- Loading state, duplicate-submission protection and preservation of entries on recoverable failure.
- Useful handling for unknown product IDs, invalid quantities, invalid/past dates, unsupported files and endpoint errors.
- Date-only logic must not shift a delivery date through UTC conversion.
- Maintain quote context through navigation; do not leak enquiry text into query strings, analytics, source files or public logs.
- Customer-facing confirmation means the actual endpoint accepted the request. It is not an order confirmation, delivery promise or binding quotation.
- Never simulate success, fabricate a quote/order number or clear the form after failed delivery.
- Separate optional unchecked marketing consent from consent to answer the enquiry.

### Delivery and privacy

Use an actual configured service/backend compatible with the selected host. Keep secrets server-side and out of the repository. Validate bot tokens server-side when used; add proportionate rate limits, request limits and spam controls. An inaccessible captcha cannot be the only route to contact.

Secure uploads need server-side type/size checks, safe filenames, private non-executable storage, appropriate scanning/provider controls and limited access. Do not render uploaded SVG/HTML or execute design files. Only accept formats the chosen system safely supports. A starting file-size limit can be chosen during implementation and documented, but must match the real endpoint. If safe uploads are unavailable, remove the upload control and provide a working, truthful alternative.

Do not activate an apparently operational form with an invented endpoint. If setup is missing, finish the interface and validation, mark integration BLOCKED, and expose only functioning contact alternatives on any public release.

During authorized testing, use clearly labelled non-sensitive test submissions and distinguish:

- UI submission attempted.
- Endpoint accepted or rejected.
- Provider queued/saved/processed.
- Notification delivered where evidence exists.
- Destination inbox receipt actually observed.

An HTTP 200 response alone is not proof of inbox receipt. Test English, French and a deliberate failure case; test the shortlist payload and upload route where enabled. Record exact evidence and limits, without public customer data.

## 14. Search engine optimization and answer clarity

SEO means search engine optimization. AEO means answer engine optimization: here it means clear, well-supported answers and understandable page structure, not hidden instructions to models.

Requirements:

- Real descriptive links, useful breadcrumbs and related content; audit whether the destination matches the label.
- One preferred HTTPS hostname and consistent URL conventions; real redirects and real 404/410 behaviour.
- No page title stuffed with a long city list and no portfolio-wide keyword footer links.
- Canonical URLs, reciprocal locale alternates, social metadata and icons reflect the final host.
- Sitemaps include only approved canonical indexable URLs, with truthful modification dates. Exclude drafts, redirects, utilities, errors, search/filter duplicates and private quote states.

## 18. Responsive quality, accessibility and performance

Target WCAG 2.2 AA through meaningful implementation and testing, without calling the site certified or fully compliant from an automated score.

Check semantic structure, skip navigation, keyboard operation, visible focus, forms and error summaries, contrast, target sizes, zoom/reflow, meaningful labels and reduced motion. Dialogs/menus/quote controls need correct focus behaviour and status announcements where appropriate.

Inspect representative templates in both languages at narrow mobile, common phone, tablet, laptop and wide desktop sizes. Useful starting viewports include approximately 360, 390, 768, 1440 and 1920 pixels; these are sampling points, not proof of every device.

Pay particular attention to:

- Long French navigation and quote buttons.
- Logo size and descriptor legibility.
- Product cards, filters, quantity inputs and shortlist controls.
- Hero crop and how much of the next section is visible.
- Sticky actions and keyboard/mobile form interaction.
- Footer, policy pages, deep links and 404 views.
- No horizontal overflow or text clipped by fixed heights.

Optimize images, fonts and scripts, reserve media dimensions and avoid layout shift. Measure representative deployed pages with available tools and current Core Web Vitals guidance. Report environment and measured results; distinguish lab tests from field data. A new site without field data cannot be declared to pass a field assessment.

Use meaningful tests for route/locale mapping, catalogue validation, shortlist payloads and important form/error behaviour. Do not create tests merely to assert decorative CSS or mirror the implementation.
